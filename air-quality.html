02 / Additional tools

Explore a wider view.

These services can help you explore pollution conditions and trends. Check the data source and whether a value is measured or modelled. For emergency decisions, use the official warning service for your area.

Map & location search

IQAir

Browse air-quality maps and search for Australian locations. Review the station or contributor details, timestamp and index used.

Open IQAir Australia ↗

Map & station readings

AQICN / WAQI

The World Air Quality Index project’s map starts in Sydney. Pan or search to explore other locations and open a station’s details.

Open the Sydney air-quality map ↗

Australian research app

AirRater

An app with air pollution, temperature and pollen information where available, and optional symptom tracking. Some areas use modelled pollution estimates where monitoring is absent.

Explore AirRater ↗
Read beyond the colour. An AQI number is an index, rather than a particle concentration in micrograms per cubic metre. Different providers can use different scales and averaging periods. Compare the pollutant, unit, location and timestamp before interpreting two readings as equivalent. Read WAQI’s explanation of index differences ↗

03 / Smoke & your health

Find advice you can act on.

NSW Health’s smoke guidance recommends checking local air quality, reducing smoke exposure and following your clinician’s advice and any existing health action plan.

Read smoke protection advice ↗

If you need help

For medical advice, call healthdirect on 1800 022 222.

For a medical emergency, including trouble breathing or chest pain or tightness, call Triple Zero (000).

04 / Understand the context

Readings show exposure.
The report asks what policy counts.

Air-quality data can tell you about pollution at a monitoring location. A fire map can show reported incidents. Neither, on its own, establishes where all smoke came from or the net benefit of a burning program.

The v2 report examines protection, smoke health costs, ecological effects, commissioning and questions for government.
